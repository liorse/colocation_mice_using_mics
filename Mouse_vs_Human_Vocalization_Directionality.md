# Mouse vs Human Vocalization Directionality Analysis

## Question
How much more directional is sound generated by mice compared to humans?

---

## Key Physical Parameters

### Mouse Vocalizations
- **Frequency Range:** 30-110 kHz (ultrasonic)
- **Wavelengths (at 343 m/s speed of sound):**
  - At 30 kHz: λ = 11.4 mm
  - At 70 kHz: λ = 4.9 mm  
  - At 110 kHz: λ = 3.1 mm
- **Source Size:** Mouse snout/aperture ~10 mm diameter (from HyVL paper)
- **Source Radius:** a ≈ 5 mm

### Human Speech
- **Frequency Range:** ~100 Hz - 8 kHz (fundamental frequencies)
- **Wavelengths:**
  - At 100 Hz: λ = 3,430 mm (3.4 m)
  - At 1 kHz: λ = 343 mm
  - At 4 kHz: λ = 86 mm
  - At 8 kHz: λ = 43 mm
- **Source Size:** Mouth opening ~20-30 mm diameter
- **Source Radius:** a ≈ 12.5 mm (average)

---

## Directionality Analysis

### Physics of Sound Directionality

Sound directionality depends on the **ka parameter**:
- **k** = 2π/λ (wave number)
- **a** = source radius
- **ka** = 2πa/λ

**Directionality behavior:**
- **ka << 1** (source << wavelength): **Omnidirectional** (point source)
- **ka ≈ 1** (source ≈ wavelength): **Moderately directional**
- **ka >> 1** (source >> wavelength): **Highly directional** (beam-like)

### Calculations

#### Mouse Vocalizations

**At 30 kHz (low end):**
- λ = 11.4 mm
- a = 5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 5 / 11.4 = 2.76**
- **Result:** Moderately directional (~3× wavelength)

**At 70 kHz (mid-range):**
- λ = 4.9 mm
- a = 5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 5 / 4.9 = 6.41**
- **Result:** Highly directional (~6× wavelength)

**At 110 kHz (high end):**
- λ = 3.1 mm
- a = 5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 5 / 3.1 = 10.1**
- **Result:** Very highly directional (~10× wavelength)

#### Human Speech

**At 100 Hz (low end):**
- λ = 3,430 mm
- a = 12.5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 12.5 / 3,430 = 0.023**
- **Result:** Nearly omnidirectional (point source)

**At 1 kHz (mid-range):**
- λ = 343 mm
- a = 12.5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 12.5 / 343 = 0.23**
- **Result:** Slightly directional but still mostly omnidirectional

**At 4 kHz (high end):**
- λ = 86 mm
- a = 12.5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 12.5 / 86 = 0.91**
- **Result:** Moderately directional (~1× wavelength)

**At 8 kHz (very high end):**
- λ = 43 mm
- a = 12.5 mm
- **ka = 2π × 12.5 / 43 = 1.83**
- **Result:** Moderately directional (~2× wavelength)

---

## Comparison Summary

| Frequency | Mouse ka | Human ka | Directionality Ratio |
|-----------|----------|----------|---------------------|
| Low (30 kHz / 1 kHz) | 2.76 | 0.23 | **~12× more directional** |
| Mid (70 kHz / 4 kHz) | 6.41 | 0.91 | **~7× more directional** |
| High (110 kHz / 8 kHz) | 10.1 | 1.83 | **~5.5× more directional** |

### Key Findings

1. **Mouse vocalizations are 5-12× more directional** than human speech across comparable frequency ranges

2. **At typical mouse vocalization frequencies (50-80 kHz):**
   - Mouse: ka ≈ 4-7 (highly directional)
   - Human speech: ka ≈ 0.3-0.7 (mostly omnidirectional)
   - **Mouse is ~10× more directional**

3. **Beam Pattern Characteristics:**
   - **Mouse:** Narrow beam pattern, significant directionality
   - **Human:** Wide radiation pattern, mostly omnidirectional at speech frequencies

---

## Practical Implications

### Why This Matters for Localization

1. **Mouse Vocalizations:**
   - **Advantage:** Directionality helps with localization using amplitude differences
   - **Challenge:** Need precise microphone positioning to capture signal
   - **Result:** Can use amplitude-based methods (as in wearable mic paper: 90% accuracy with amplitude alone)

2. **Human Speech:**
   - **Advantage:** Omnidirectional radiation makes it easier to capture from any angle
   - **Challenge:** Less amplitude variation between microphones, relies more on TDOA
   - **Result:** Primarily uses time-difference-of-arrival (TDOA) methods

### Evidence from Research

**From "Mountable Miniature Microphones" paper:**
- **90% accuracy** assigning mouse vocalizations using **amplitude differences alone**
- This high accuracy with amplitude suggests significant directionality
- Combined with video tracking: **97% accuracy**

**From HyVL paper:**
- Physical limit of localization: **mouse snout ~10 mm**
- Achieved precision: **3.4-4.8 mm** (approaching physical limits)
- This precision is possible partly because of the directional nature of the source

## Beam Width Estimates

### Approximate Beam Width Formula
For a circular aperture: **θ ≈ 1.22 × λ / D** (first null)

Where:
- θ = beam width (half-angle to first null)
- λ = wavelength
- D = aperture diameter

### Mouse Vocalizations

**At 70 kHz (typical):**
- λ = 4.9 mm
- D = 10 mm
- **θ ≈ 1.22 × 4.9 / 10 = 0.60 radians ≈ 34°**
- **Beam width ≈ 68°** (total angle)

**At 110 kHz:**
- λ = 3.1 mm
- D = 10 mm
- **θ ≈ 1.22 × 3.1 / 10 = 0.38 radians ≈ 22°**
- **Beam width ≈ 44°** (total angle)

### Human Speech

**At 4 kHz (typical high frequency):**
- λ = 86 mm
- D = 25 mm (average mouth opening)
- **θ ≈ 1.22 × 86 / 25 = 4.2 radians ≈ 240°**
- **Beam width ≈ 480°** (essentially omnidirectional)

**At 8 kHz:**
- λ = 43 mm
- D = 25 mm
- **θ ≈ 1.22 × 43 / 25 = 2.1 radians ≈ 120°**
- **Beam width ≈ 240°** (still very wide)

---

## Conclusion

**Mouse vocalizations are approximately 5-12× more directional than human speech**, depending on frequency:

- **At typical mouse frequencies (50-80 kHz):** ~10× more directional
- **Beam width:** Mouse ~44-68° vs Human ~240-480° (essentially omnidirectional)
- **Practical impact:** Mouse vocalizations can be localized using amplitude differences alone with high accuracy (90-97%), while human speech localization relies primarily on TDOA methods

This directionality is a key factor enabling the high-precision localization systems described in your research papers, allowing millimeter-level precision (3.4-4.8 mm) that approaches the physical limits of the mouse snout size (~10 mm).
